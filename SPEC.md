# Product Spec v0.4: Easy Quizy

A vocabulary app for German learners.

## 1. Context

**Problem.** The author is at B1-B2 German. The bottleneck is vocabulary: it blocks speaking, writing and understanding people. Vocabulary is the fastest lever, and cognitive science says how to pull it: active recall and spaced repetition (the Anki approach).

**Why not just Anki.** Anki asks you to choose a deck and a level up front, and it feels like admin. Easy Quizy has no level picker and no deck. You start with one word, words appear by association, and the app works out where your knowledge ends.

**Scope for v1.** A personal tool for the author, German only, English as the bridge language, iOS first. It should be possible to hand to Android-using friends later without a rewrite.

## 2. Principles

1. **One gesture everywhere.** The Circle is used to rate a word in Explore and in Study.
2. **Minimal setup.** No level, no deck. The app asks for a name once and learns the rest from the first fifteen words (adaptive calibration, section 8), goes straight into learning them, then keeps every word at the right difficulty.
3. **Recall over recognition.** You commit to an answer before you see it.
4. **Never overwhelm.** New words wait in a pile and enter Study in small batches.
5. **Words in context, but concise.** Gender (through the article), plural, an example sentence and audio belong to the word, but the reveal must be concise and easy to take in at a glance. The user decides what the reveal shows (see Settings).

## 3. The Circle (core interaction)

- One word sits in a circle in the center of the screen. Nouns always show their article: **das Haus**. The article already conveys gender, so there is **no separate gender color-coding**.
- **There is no visible ring.** The user draws a circle round the word with a finger, and the app tracks it along an invisible ring (the drawing may start anywhere round the word: on the word, on the ring, or a little outside it; only the exact centre does not count. Usability tests showed people could not find an invisible ring). It can be drawn **either way round**, clockwise or counter-clockwise; a full circle means coming back to where you started. The app tracks how far the finger has turned around the circle's centre, so it works from any starting point.
- **Stardust.** Stars appear where the finger goes (up to 210 of them along the circle), each settling in from slightly outside and then drifting and twinkling in its own way, with a soft glow at the finger. **The colour does not go from red to green; the rating shows as how much the stars shine**, in clear steps that match the hidden segments: "don't know" is a few small but clear **blue** stars with a soft blue glow; "hard" a little bigger and brighter, an icy blue-white; "good" warm cream with an orange glow; "know" large golden-white stars in a strong orange-gold glow. The user rates by feel, not by picking a named level.
- **When the finger lets go early**, the ring carries on by itself to the end (about 1.5 s), in the same colour and brightness the finger stopped at: the rating is carried by how bright the stars are, not by whether the ring is complete.
- **Hidden segments.** The user never sees them, but the arc is divided into four:

| Segment | Share of the arc | Meaning in Explore | Grade in Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | first 30% | don't know it | Again |
| 2 | next 30% | vaguely familiar | Hard |
| 3 | next 30% | know it, still want to study | Good |
| 4 ("know") | last 10% | know it for sure | Easy |

- **Haptic ticks between the sections.** A light tick as the arc crosses from one hidden section into the next (at 30% and at 60%), and a firmer tick with the visual snap to a full ring when it enters the last 10% ("know") section. Ticks fire when drawing back across a boundary too, with a small buffer so a finger resting on a boundary does not buzz. Only the last section snaps visually, and there is **no visual hint** for any of the sections.
- **A plain tap (no drag) does nothing.**
- **What the rating does in Explore:**
  - In the "know" segment: the word is treated as known and is not added to the pile (silent spot-checks later).
  - In any other segment: the word goes to the pile, and the exact continuous value is kept for scheduling.
- **Discoverability:** the gesture is taught on a screen of its own before the test (see section 8): the first word, *Feierabend*, with a ghost finger, the line "draw a circle around the word to show how well you know it, from not knowing at all to knowing it well", and after the first drawing a "great job" note with captions on the small things (the dot on the ring that changes the answer, flipping the word). Nowhere else is the drawing explained.
- **Changing the answer (replaces undo).** There is no undo. The moment the finger lets go, **a handle appears on the ring** (with one soft pulse), at the end of what was drawn, and the ring then completes itself as before; the translation shows in the circle. The handle is a small dot **in the colour of the stars**, so it takes the colour of the answer (blue for "don't know", icy for "hard", cream for "good", golden white for "know"). **Taking the handle makes the part the ring drew by itself disappear at once**: only the arc up to the handle stays. Dragging it round the ring (either way, with the same haptic ticks as drawing) raises or lowers the answer, and the stars shine less or more as it goes. **When the finger lets go, the new answer counts and the ring completes itself again** (a known answer flares, as when it is drawn), so it is clear that the answer was taken. Swiping up anywhere else still moves on (the handle is a touch area of its own); **while the handle is held, the swipe up to the next word ignores the drag**, so dragging the handle upwards round the ring does not send the word away. All the way down is the same as the cross. Nothing can be un-rated: a wrong rating is lowered with the handle, and a word that was marked known goes back to "added to study" by dragging the handle under the "know" line.
  - **Two answers are saved for every word:** the first (`confidence`, drawn before the meaning was seen) and the answer after it was changed (`value`). Explore keeps them in the rating (`confidence` next to `value`; older saves have no `confidence`), Study in `reviewLog` (`confidence`, `value`, time) in the profile. What counts for the app is the changed answer: Explore's known / added to study, the level estimate and the Study schedule (FSRS reviews the word again from its earlier card with the new value). The first answer is for measuring how sure people are before they see the answer.
  - **In Explore a word cannot be raised into "known" after the meaning is shown** (seeing it makes every word look familiar, and a known word is never studied): the handle stops at the top of "good" (0.89), or at where the first answer was, if that was higher (`adjustCeiling`). Lowering is free. In Study the handle goes the whole way both ways.
  - **Hint:** one text everywhere, "hint: if you were wrong, drag the dot on the ring to change your initial answer", on a plate of its own (dark card, border) **directly above the circle**, just clear of the handle at the top of the ring: on the first test word in Explore (it fades in after the badge has gone, about 2.3 s) and in the Study test on the first word ever studied. **It shows once only** (that first word in each place). The practice screen has its own wording as its main text (see section 8).
- **Moving on (two ways, one motion):**
  - **Tap any similar word** below the circle to go to it. Words you have already rated are not shown as chips.
  - **Swipe up** to keep going: it follows the current word's associations first (the first unrated related word), then falls back to the trail order.
  - Both use the same motion: the word glides up and away, and the next one rises in from below and grows into place. Weak or short swipes spring back. There is no "next" button.
- **Layout.** The circle sits a little above the middle of the screen so the example sentence and similar words have room below it.
- **Flip the word.** After rating, tap anywhere inside the circle (the tap area is round, like the circle) to see the original German word again (with its article, exactly as before rating), and tap again to get the translation back. It resets to the translation on every new word.
- **Status plate.** After you rate a word, a small plate appears **at the top of the screen, in the same place on every screen** (the first-run screens and Explore), under the header: the **badge** (`RatingBadge`), which says what the word's answer is now ("added to study" or "marked as known"). It shows for **two seconds** after the answer is given, follows the handle while the answer is being changed (and shows again for two seconds when what it says changes), then fades away. **The Study test has no badge** (there the ring and its handle are enough). Tapping the translation in the circle flips it to the German word and back (also in Study).
- **A known word glows and sends stardust up.** Marking a word as known makes the ring flare and glow orange-gold all round (it lights up at once and dies away in about a second) while a crowd of small stars (about 170, in waves) comes loose from the ring and drifts up like fireflies, steadily, out of the top of the screen (about 3.6 s). The glow is gone well before the last star has left, and the ring is then simply a full golden ring, which it stays for as long as the word is shown. (This replaces the old star and wave of light across the screen, which testers did not read as "a star lit up".) It is drawn on a layer above the whole app, only while the stardust is on its way.
- **Hear the word (audio).** A small **loudspeaker icon sits in the circle, above the word, always in the same place** (just clear of the article above a noun, so it is in the same place for every word), in the same quiet style as the cross under the word (grey, 20 px, in a 40 px touch area). Tapping it says the German word aloud (a noun with its article, "der Feierabend", so the gender is heard too) with **the voice of the phone** (`expo-speech`, German, slightly slower than normal: nothing is recorded or downloaded). It is on Explore, the first-run test, the practice screen and the Study test, and also on the word card (above the article); it is hidden while a circle is being drawn; once the word is rated the translation is in the circle and **the icon is gone**; tapping the circle to see the German word again brings it back (it is only there while the German word is). A new word stops the one being said. On a real iPhone nothing is heard while the **silent switch** is on. Not built: a recorded voice, automatic playing.
- **Tips.** Only one is left: on the first three words of the test a small, quiet "swipe up for the next word" shows after rating.
- **In Study,** typed answers are auto-graded. The circle animates to the grade the app decided (for example, a typo lands in the "Good" segment). The user can override by drawing.

## 4. The three tabs (in this order)

### 4.1 Explore (home)
- An endless feed of words, one at a time, each rated with the Circle.
- **The way of exploring (built, replaces the old lens idea):** the header row says "explore" at the left, and **next to the title are selects on glass pills** (the next ones go on to a second line when they do not fit): the way of exploring (**"by association"** by default), only when exploring by theme the topic (**"in travel"**), the level and the kind of words (see below); the little word ("by", "in") is grey, the choice white. Tapping a select opens its menu right under it. Four ways: **association** (follows the words linked to the one on screen), **theme** (stays in one topic; the topic is its own select, "by theme | in travel", and a tap on it picks another; "explore from here" on the Map sets it), **usefulness** (the most common words first) and **free** (any word at the user's level, no connections, a little random). **There is no limit:** the user can explore any number of words and study whenever they like (the old pause at 20 waiting words is gone). **Filters** sit behind a filled funnel icon at the end of the title row: level (easier, my level, harder: moves the target tier by one) and words (all, nouns, verbs, adj & adv). **Interests** (chosen once, in onboarding; no longer in settings): themes the user likes, which association and usefulness prefer when there are words at the right level. **Changing the way of exploring or a filter changes the word on screen too:** a word that has not been rated yet and does not suit the new settings glides away and one that does takes its place (a word that already fits stays; a word just rated is left alone, and the next one follows the new settings). All of it keeps to the user's level and has no randomness. Word families and "words that go together" are not built yet.
- **Lens** (the user's chosen way of exploring), one selectable control:
  - **Themes**: words from the same situation (Küche → Herd, Pfanne, Spülmaschine)
  - **Word families**: fahren → Fahrer, Fahrzeug, Abfahrt
  - **Most useful**: the frequency ladder, filling the most valuable gaps first
  - **In sentences**: words that co-occur (Entscheidung + treffen)
- **Words always fit the user.** The association trail decides *which area* to explore, and the user's estimated level decides *which words within it*: not too easy, not too hard (see "Calibration" in section 8). The estimate keeps updating from every rating: many high ratings push toward rarer words, many low ratings ease back toward common ones. **Pure Wander** (follow the trail regardless of difficulty) is an option in **Settings**, off by default.
- **Level and words are selects** next to the title ("my level" / "easier" / "harder"; "all words" / "nouns" / "verbs" / "adj & adv"). During the first-run test it is the counter instead (below). The profile is the fourth tab (section 5).
- **Counter.** Only during the first-run test: a mini ring and "n / 15". Afterwards there is no counter and no pause. The similar-word chips only offer words within one tier of the user's level, so they are never far too easy or too hard.
- **Word graph rule.** Semantically close pairs (opposites, near-synonyms) are not introduced back to back, to avoid interference. Theme and word-family links are preferred for new material.
- **First word for everyone:** *Feierabend* (a delightful, very German word). After roughly ten words, the first stars appear on the Map.

### 4.2 Study
- **The tab opens on a list of the words you are learning** (gathered, not yet learned; the title row holds three small icons on the right: a sort icon whose menu holds the ways to sort (status, a-z, newest), a magnifier for search, and a plus to add a word of your own; a bin on each row deletes a word for good: it asks "are you sure?" in a window first). **Each row shows the word's state as a circle** (our metaphor: empty = new, a little filled = shaky, more = getting there, full = learned), not as a label. **Until a word has been studied (it has no card yet) its circle follows how well the user said they knew it** (under a quarter: new; to a half: shaky; to three quarters: getting there; above: solid; a full circle drawn: learned); after studying, the card decides. Tapping a word opens its full card (the status circle (no label) at the top left and the cross at the top right; then **only the word and its meaning above one thin line**: a loudspeaker, the article small and close above the word, the word (**32 when it fits the card, 24 when it does not**, smaller only for a very long word), the meaning in the light ink; **everything else under the line**: for a verb its forms on one line, "geht · ging · ist gegangen" (he/she/it present, past, perfect), the plural, **the words it often comes with, as chips like the similar words in Explore: tapping one opens that word in the Map tab** (the Map opens its topic, then the page with the topic's words, and the word's own card on it; a word without a topic is a chip that does nothing), your note, and up to two **examples**, and **last of all the topic** (the name of the theme, with a small label) ("how common" is no longer shown); the "together" words and extra examples are computed from the Tatoeba sentences by `scripts/words/extra_info.py` into `src/data/words.extra.ts`); **the card can be swiped up for the next word of the list and down for the previous one** (the same motion as in Explore; a small nudge the first time says so); **when the card holds more than fits (many examples, a verb's forms, a note) it scrolls inside itself instead, and pulling it past its bottom or top goes to the next or previous card**. The card closes with **a cross in a small round button at the top right of the card** (in the top row of the card, opposite the status circle at the left; there is no chevron row above the card any more, so the card is taller) or with a tap outside it; no counter. Below the list sit the session buttons (see below). When no session is ready (fewer than 3 new words are waiting and no review is due) it is disabled, with a line saying how many are gathered. Every time the tab is opened it starts on the list again.
- **Your own words (the plus):** opens a small page (chevron back, "add new word"): the german word (an article in front, "das Fahrrad", is split off; **no keyboard opens by itself (neither here nor on the name screen: the user taps into a field), and while it is up the button sits right above it so the form is not cropped**; **while it is typed, words of our list that match are offered, and a tap fills the whole form in; adding a word that is already in the list puts that word in your study words instead of making a copy**), the english translation, the **part of speech** (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, other; guessed from an article or a "to ..." meaning until the user picks one; the Explore filter "words" uses it), an example in each language (optional), a **note of your own** (optional, multi-line; the placeholder asks what it is confused with, for a sentence of your own, how it connects to your life; it shows on the word card as "your note"), the topic (a drop-down of ours) and how well you know it already (not at all, a little, quite well, very well; shown with the status circles). "add word" needs the german word and the translation; the word joins the list like a gathered one, at the status chosen, is kept on the phone and shows on the Map under its topic.
- **One button: "study"** (no number under it). **How many words a session holds is a setting (`sessionWords`): 10, 20 (the default) or 40**, with about how long each takes (a word takes five or six seconds: draw, read, swipe; about 1, 2 or 4 minutes). It is asked once in the first-run path (after the interests) and can be changed in the profile; it replaces the old "quick sessions" switch (saved profiles with that switch on become 10, the others 20). The size counts **reviews and new words together** and is a ceiling, not a promise: the reviews that are due come first (most overdue first), but the new words keep a **share of 40%** when many reviews are due, and take all the room the reviews leave when there are few; with fewer words waiting the session is shorter. It is the length of one go, not a daily limit: another session can be started at once.
- **The test looks exactly like Explore:** the same header (title "study", a grey counter "n / 5" on the right), the same circle in the same place and size, the German word in it; you draw how well you know it, the English appears, the example sentence under it in German with its English translation. No instructions above or below. **A swipe up moves on**, as in Explore.
- **Not knowing a word at all is one tap:** a small cross inside the circle, right under the word (it goes away as soon as a circle is started; Explore, the first-run test, the practice screen, the Study test), rates the word at the lowest value without drawing; the ring then draws itself all the way round, in dark blue.
- **The prompt type changes with how well the word is known** (later): recognition with the Circle first; then cloze, then typing.
- **Changing the answer in the test too:** after a word is rated in the Study test, there is no badge, and the handle on the ring works as in section 3. When the finger lets go the review is done again from the word's earlier card with the new value, so the schedule always follows the answer after the meaning was shown.
- **Celebration:** a word rated above 75% in Study gives a success haptic.
- **When the batch is done:** "nicely done!" and "the key is in daily practice, so come back to your words regularly", and nothing else but the **daily reminder** (a switch, and the time of day when it is on); the tab bar is the way on.
- **A session needs at least 3 new words waiting** (or a review that is due). The 20 that pauses Explore is only the ceiling, not a gate.
- Words are **not** drip-fed daily. They enter only when a session begins.

### 4.3 Map
- A **night sky** for motivation and overview. The Map is on the same starry paper as every other screen (deep navy `#0C1927`; the small stars behind it are the app's own, filling up as words are rated), so that glowing stars can read as light.
- **It looks like a starred sky with a web of threads.** The small background stars are the app's own starry paper (the same as on every screen, filling up as words are rated). Each theme is **one bigger star** with four thin rays and its English name under it (**names never overlap**: a name goes under its star, or above or beside it when that spot is taken, chosen by `placeLabels`; a test checks it on the real sky for several phone sizes and sky seeds) (24 in the current list: the five small topics of the first prototype, work, getting around, kitchen, home and decisions and feelings, were folded into the big topics work and business, travel, food and drink, house and furniture, and ideas and plans / feelings and character / time and calendar). A theme with more words is a slightly bigger star, but only a little and with a cap. **Related themes are joined by thin lines**, from a **hand-made list of neighbours in meaning** (`src/data/themeLinks.ts`: kitchen next to food and drink and to house and furniture, weather next to animals and environment, and so on; the links that fall out of the words' own related lists were too noisy). The stars are placed so that neighbours in meaning are neighbours on screen, **and the easy themes sit at the top left and the hard ones at the bottom right** (a hand-set level per theme, 1 to 5, in the same file): **no two stars sit close to each other** (a least distance of about 0.8 of the space each star has, kept before and, a little looser, after each sky's own nudge; a test checks it); **the arrangement is the same for everyone** (the best of 16 pulls-together of the linked stars, stretched to fill the sky). **Every user still gets a sky of their own:** a random seed is chosen once on the first launch (and again if onboarding restarts); it nudges every star a little and decides where the small stars fall. The map is never mirrored, so the corners always mean the same. **A line is drawn only between two stars that each have at least one explored word** (never to a theme you have not touched); it is faint and cool at first, and warm and brighter where both stars shine.
- **A star shines as more of its theme is taken, in steps by share (so it keeps working when a theme has thousands of words).** The share is the part of the theme's words the user has taken (met and rated). **Under 1%** the star is still dark: a small dim dot with no halo, no rays, a quiet name; **every dark star looks exactly the same** (one size, one steady brightness, whatever the theme holds). The size differences between themes show only once a star has started to shine. **From 1% to 10%** it is the same warm point as a glowing star, with only a tiny glow and no rays. **From 10%** it starts to glow, and then more and more: about 25% a small halo with rays, 50% clearly bright, 75% large, all words a full blaze (larger core, wider orange halo, longer rays, a soft nebula behind it). So a new user's map is quiet and dim: mostly dark stars and a few yellow points. A word is *known* once it was marked known in Explore, or its FSRS card lasts 21+ days (this still decides how the words of a theme look inside its sphere).
- **The sky is bigger than the screen (about 2 screens wide and 2 tall).** The Map opens already zoomed in on the middle of it, so stars run off the edges, and the user **drags in any direction to look around** (a flick keeps gliding to a stop, and it stops at the edge of the sky). The sky fills the whole screen, behind the header, the info card and the tab bar, so there are no bare bands at the top or bottom. Under the header one dim line explains the sky: "every star is a topic. it lights up as you learn its words." It hides while a theme is open.
- **Tap a big star, or pinch in on it once the camera is already close, and the sky zooms in on it** (pinching zooms into the point between the fingers, not the middle of the map; pinching out on an open theme closes it, following the fingers; the camera has three rests: the whole sky, the sky close up, and one theme). The rest of the sky grows and dims (the other big stars fade out) and the star slides to the middle, where the theme opens as **a slowly turning sphere of dots** (fine dust gives it its shape, the near side brighter and bigger than the far side). **Each word of the theme is one point of the sphere** (an even sample beyond 60 words), scattered over it so none clump. **The words you have taken into study shine** (orange while being learned, warm yellow once known); words you have not met are dim dots. The more words are learned, the more of the sphere shines. Drawn by the same shader as the big stars. Inspired by the dotted "thinking orbs" of libraries.dev, redrawn on our own Skia, no code taken from there.
- **A small card below the cluster** shows the theme's name in English and how many of its words are explored (the German name of the theme is not shown), with two buttons, one above the other: the main action on top and **see words** (quieter) under it. **The words are one level further in:** tapping the sphere itself, pinching further in on it, or "see words" opens a page of its own with all the words of the topic (**the words you have explored come first, the one explored latest on top, then the words not met yet**; the same rows as in Study, with the status circle; tap one to open its card, which swipes like in Study; a chevron goes back to the sphere). The main action sits on that page too. There is no other breakdown by state.
- **When every word of a theme is explored**, the card says so ("all the words in this cluster are already explored, so it's time to study them") and the button becomes **study**, which goes to the Study tab.
- **One action:** **explore from here** on that card switches to Explore, which starts on an unexplored word of that theme closest to the user's level. The button reads "all explored" when nothing is left. Tapping the sky zooms back out (the card has no "close" label). It is not a full navigation surface.
- The header shows only how many words have been explored ("25 words", plain text), never the total, so how many words the app holds stays a surprise. **The whole list of words lives in Study; the Map shows only the words of the open topic.** The first time the Map tab is opened (and it never comes back after leaving the tab or tapping "got it"), a card (never over an opened topic, its word list or a word's card) says what it is (every star is a topic, it lights up as you learn its words; tap a star, pinch, drag) with a "got it" button.
- Clusters follow **themes**. Word-family clusters are not built yet (the family links in the data are only strings, not connections between words).

## 5. Profile and settings

**The fourth tab** of the bottom bar (Explore, Study, Map, Profile; a head-and-shoulders icon), titled "profile" like the other tabs. Credits opens one level down from it, with a chevron back.
- **Profile:** the avatar (the initial), the name (tap to change it), and three numbers: words explored, in study, learned (under the week of stars). Above them (right under the name) **the week, as seven glowing round stars** (each a **crisp round core with a sharp edge** and, in it, a white heart that has no edge (brightest in the middle, fading into the colour of the core), with a **soft blurred glow round it that fades out into the dark: the blur is only at the edges**, not in the middle; a day with study twinkles gently) (on a plate like the other cards of the profile: frosted, with a border and rounded corners; no title, no line of text; only the letters m t w t f s s right under the stars, today's letter bright; the stars are **all the same size** (only the shine changes), **the row fills the plate inside its side padding of 16, the same as the other cards** (the first star at the left of that padding, the last at the right) **and the gaps between the stars are all equal**; the glow may reach past the plate). One star for each day, Monday first, in **the same shine as the circle's stardust**: a day without study is a small **dark blue** star, quiet; the more of the day's goal is done the more it shines (icy blue, then cream, with a wider glow) up to a **bright golden star with an orange glow** when the goal is done. **The goal of a day is the number of words in a session** (the setting under it): the words reviewed in Study that day against that number. The days to come are dimmer still. **Studying counts in full; meeting words in Explore counts too, for half as much**: a day of only exploring lights the star up to the middle of the scale (cream, never golden: gold is for studying), a day of studying goes all the way, and a bit of both adds up. **There is no streak that burns down and no counter** (a streak and a "17 / 10 words" line were built first and dropped on 2026-10-20: a number that resets to zero puts guilt where the app wants calm): a missed day just stays a dark star. The days come from the review log and, for words studied before the log existed, the last review of their card (that old review counts as one word).
- **Daily reminder** (built): a switch and a time (default 19:00); after the time is changed a **"save"** button appears (turning the switch off saves at once). Its line says "a quiet nudge, only when your words are ready to be practiced". It is a local notification, set by the app each time it goes to the background: **one** reminder, on the first day when something is ready (reviews that are due, or at least 3 new words waiting), and none if nothing will be ready. It stays quiet while the app is open. It is offered on the "nicely done" screen after a finished session (the same switch and time as here); the phone's permission is asked when the switch is turned on.
- **Credits** (a page one level down, opened from a row "credits" under "about"): where the words and meanings (Wiktionary, via Wiktextract and kaikki.org; CC BY-SA 4.0), the sentences (Tatoeba; CC BY 2.0 FR), the word frequencies (FrequencyWords by Hermit Dave, from OpenSubtitles; CC BY-SA 4.0), the scheduling (FSRS, ts-fsrs; MIT), the type (Satoshi; ITF Free Font License) and the libraries come from, with a tappable link to each. A note says the word list is shared under the same share-alike terms.
- **Words per session** (10, 20 or 40): the size of a Study session (section 4.2). A card with the title and, under it, about how long the chosen size takes, and a **select** on the right ("20 words" with a chevron). Tapping it opens a **panel that slides up from the bottom** over everything (on a dimmed screen, with a grabber at the top) with the three sizes as tiles and a **save** button. Nothing changes until "save"; a tap outside the panel or dragging it down closes it and leaves the setting as it was. Asked first in the first-run path, there as the same three tiles on a screen of its own.
- **Start over** (a testing aid, to remove before release).
- **Not built yet:** what the reveal card shows (meaning, plural, example, word family chips, memory hook), Wander or Guided exploring.

## 6. Card and content model

- **Unit:** single words, one card per **sense** (Bank as bench and Bank as bank are separate). Phrases and collocations are a later addition, so the data model must be able to hold them.
- **Reflexive verbs always carry "sich"** in their lemma ("sich ernähren", "sich konzentrieren", "sich setzen", ...), with an example sentence that uses the reflexive pronoun; the add-word search finds them with or without "sich". (20 verbs were converted from their bare form, including "sich erinnern" and "sich vorstellen"; a saved rating or card under the old id is carried over.)
- **Fields per entry:** lemma, sense id, part of speech, article, gender, plural, **for a verb its forms (present and past of he/she/it, perfect with its auxiliary; step 9 of the word pipeline, `words.verbs.ts`)**, English gloss, frequency rank, example sentence(s) with audio, word-family links, association links (`related`: the words you can travel to), theme tags, co-occurrence links, memory hook.
- **Dataset size:** 3,000-5,000 senses for the full first version. **Now 1,474 words** (33 hand-written, 1,441 from the pipeline in `scripts/words/`, see its README): tiers from a real frequency list, article, plural and meaning from Wiktionary, examples from Tatoeba and Wiktionary, themes chosen by hand. The data is licensed CC BY-SA / CC BY 2.0 FR, so a credits screen is needed before release.
- **Data source (pre-built dataset bundled in the app):**
  - Frequency lists for ranking
  - Wiktionary extracts for senses, gender, plural and word families
  - Tatoeba for example sentences
  - TTS for audio (built: the voice of the phone, see section 3; a recorded voice later)
  - An LLM-assisted, human-checked step for themes, links and memory hooks
  - **Check licenses and attribution** (Wiktionary and Tatoeba are share-alike or attribution licenses)
- No live LLM in v1. No backend.

## 7. Scheduling

- Spaced repetition with **FSRS** (built: the `ts-fsrs` library, wrapped in `src/srs/scheduler.ts`). Intervals are whole days (no minutes-long learning steps) and there is no fuzz.
- **A Study batch** is the reviews that are due (up to 10, most overdue first) followed by up to 5 new words from the pile (rated lowest first). Reviews have no card: they only appear in the Circle part.
- **Seed the initial state from the Circle value** in Explore. A word rated near green starts with a longer first interval than one rated near red. This is a departure from Anki, which starts every card the same.
- The four hidden segments map onto the four standard grades (Again / Hard / Good / Easy) for the scheduler.
- **Spot-checks of "known" words (proposal):**
  - A known word is scheduled like any other card, but with a high starting stability, so its first check comes after roughly 3 weeks.
  - At most 1-2 spot-checks are mixed into a session, and they look like ordinary cards (no special screen).
  - If the user passes, the next check moves far out (months).
  - If the user fails, the word moves quietly into the pile, with a light note on the reveal ("Added to your pile") and no penalty message.
- Prompt type advances with word strength, from recognition to production.

## 8. Onboarding, reminders

- **First launch, in order** (a **line of stars** at the top of steps 2 to 7 shows how far along the user is: the circle's stardust laid out flat, a thin band two or three stars deep (clusters, as on the ring, not a single row), the stars up to the current step shining cream with a warm glow and a light at the front, with room for the glow at both ends so it is never cut off, the rest dim points; it fills smoothly as the screens change): (1) **welcome**: a falling star drawn above "easy quizy words", the line "learning words can be the most boring part of learning a language. but with easy quizy it turns into an explorative journey, which you will want to come back to." and "let's begin"; (2) **the name**: "let's get to know each other first"; (3) **how it works** (straight after the name): "Name, here is how easy quizy works" (the name in front) and the three steps (each number in its circle sits level with the middle of its text block), *explore: meet new words through our algorithm adjusted to your level / study: learn the ones you don't know with acknowledged spaced repetition method / map: watch your vocabulary grow on a night sky map*; (4) **the way of exploring**: "how would you want to explore new words?", "you can change it any time" and four choices, the ways of exploring of Explore with the same lines (association: follows the words that go with the one you see; theme: stays in one topic; usefulness: the most common words first; free: any word at your level, no connections), each a row with a radio on the right (association is chosen already); the choice is where Explore starts (the first select of its title). It replaced the interests step (topics as chips), which is gone: nothing sets interests any more, and Explore still prefers the topics of anyone who had chosen some; (5) **the session size**: "how many words per session?", "you can change this later in your profile" and three tiles (10, 20 or 40 words, with about 1, 2 or 4 minutes under each; 20 is chosen already); (6) **a practice screen** with *Feierabend* only, in three moments. The texts sit **in the middle of the space between the line of stars and the circle**, left-aligned like the other first-run screens, and **the circle is exactly where it is in Explore's test** (so it does not jump when the practice ends). First "let's begin with your first word" and "rate how well you know the word by drawing a circle around it. if you don't know it at all, tap the cross" (the cross goes away as soon as a circle is started); then, once the word is rated (the meaning shows in the circle and the handle sits on the ring; **no badge here, the title says it**; **the first text fades out completely, and only after a beat the new one fades in**, so the change is clear), a title that follows the **first** answer ("it's your first word to study!", or "wow, you already know it!" for a known word) and does not change when the handle is dragged afterwards and, in the ordinary subtext style, "if you feel like your initial evaluation was wrong, you can drag the handle and adjust it. try it", and **no "continue" yet: the handle has to be used first**; when it has been let go and the ring has finished drawing itself again (about 1.7 s) the circle fades away and the title "great job! you will explore words and study them this way" shows with the subtext "words that you don't remember well will be presented for studying earlier than those you know well" and "continue" (the rating is not saved); (7) **the quick-test screen**: "let us calibrate to your level", "you will see 15 words, show us how well you know them" and "start the test"; (8) the 15 test words in Explore (counter top right); the 15th word has no "swipe up" tip; (9) "got it, Name" (no full stop) and "you will explore new words that match your level. but studying them is the key, so let's practice a bit on the words you just gathered" with "study".
- **Calibration (the first 15 words).** There is no separate test screen: the opening words are ordinary Explore words, rated with the Circle. But they are chosen adaptively to find where the user's knowledge ends.
  - **The words are not the same in every test:** the first one comes from the middle of the ladder (tier 3), and every pick is a random one among the words that fit the estimate equally well (so two tests of the same kind of person share only a few words); a theme not yet met is still preferred. Simulated, a person who knows everything up to tier 3 is estimated at 3.5 (tier 4 as the target) after 15 words; one who knows up to tier 5 at 5.5.
  - **Words are picked by their real place on the frequency list (their rank), not by tier** (since 2026-10-19). The estimate is still a fractional level 1-6, but it is turned into a rank (between the middles of the tiers: 245, 1,040, 2,680, 5,660, 11,300, 28,300) and the word closest to it on a log scale is chosen. Before, a tier-2 word of rank 15,000 could come right after the user said they did not know a common word (a tier holds words from very different ranks, and words chosen by hand got tiers that do not match their rank). Words that are not on the frequency list (rank 50,000 and up, 49 of them) are left out of the test. The steps are 1.5 shrinking by 0.8 per answer, and "know" and "don't know" move the estimate equally far. Tests check that after a "don't know" the next word is never harder. **Explore after the test uses the same measure** (`difficultyRank` in `calibration.ts`, `feed.ts`): a word fits the user when its rank is within about one tier of the level (a factor of 2.7) on a log scale, "easier" / "harder" move the level by one tier, "usefulness" (the most common words first) skips words that are hopelessly easy (more than about 7 times more common than the level) as well as the too hard ones, and "explore from here" on the Map starts at the word of the topic nearest to the level. A word that is not on the frequency list counts as being as common as the middle of its tier.
  - The words come from a **frequency ladder**, and each rating moves the next pick. A high rating (know it) jumps to a rarer word. A low rating (don't know) steps back to a more common one. Big jumps at first, smaller steps as the estimate settles, like a binary search.
  - **No similar-word chips and no association trail during calibration.** Words linked by association sit in one thematic zone, which would make the estimate read one area of knowledge instead of the level. Instead the picks are spread across themes (ties go to a theme not yet met), and the user just swipes up for the next word. The screen shows a small progress count ("3 / 15").
  - The first word carries a greeting above the circle: "let’s roll, Name" / "here is your first word", the same distance above the circle as the tip sits below it. The background is still an empty navy: the first star appears when this word is rated.
  - The result is a **level estimate** (a position on the frequency ladder plus how sure the app is). The estimate keeps refining after calibration ends, from every later rating.
  - **The user is told what it did, the moment the 15th word is rated** (after a short beat, so the ring's snap is not cut off): "got it, Name. we adjusted the words you’ll explore so they match your level." No score, no level name, no swipe needed. The words themselves then prove it.
  - Words rated during calibration count like any other: they go to the pile or are marked as known.
  - **Straight into Study.** The onboarding does not end in Explore. When the 15th word is rated, the message above appears and the user is taken **directly into a first Study session** to learn the words they just gathered, so the first minutes already deliver the app's real loop (find words, then learn them).
    - The session is the usual small batch: **up to 5 words from the pile**, the ones the user rated lowest first (the ones they most need), first as cards, then in the Circle (the same two parts as any session). It is a normal Study session in every way (12-20 card badge does not apply, it ends when the batch is done), with a friendly closing line and a choice: "keep going" or "back to Explore".
    - Study is where the Circle's value seeds the scheduler (section 7), so the calibration ratings directly set the first review intervals.
    - **Few words in the pile:** if the user knew most of the 15 (only 1-2 in the pile), do not force a tiny session. Say so ("you know a lot already"), keep exploring further up the ladder, and start Study once at least 3 words have gathered.
    - The tab bar is hidden until the 15 words are rated and **appears with the first Study visit** (the tabs open on Study, on the list of words), so the user can leave at any moment. Later launches open on Explore.
  - **Edge cases:** if the ratings are inconsistent, keep the estimate wide and keep probing a bit longer. If the user rates everything as known or everything as unknown, move to the far end of the ladder quickly, then settle.
- **Reminders:** one gentle daily nudge at a time the user chooses, only if something is waiting.

## 10. Visual direction

- **A deep navy night, everywhere.** The whole app is dark: background `#0C1927`, **at the start the sky is empty, plain navy.** Every word the user rates, whatever the rating (known or to study), lights one more small star somewhere on the background of every screen (with a soft ring that spreads and fades on the new one), so the further they get, the more stars there are. The stars are the same evenly scattered small ones as on the Map, in a scrambled order so the sky fills up all over, not from a corner. The first stars are a little brighter and bigger so each one is noticed; as the sky fills they settle to a small, dim level (after about 80 stars), so the background never competes with the content. Undoing a rating removes its star. The Map draws its own, full sky with the big theme stars on top of it.
- **Ink and greys are cool and light:** text and icons `#EFF2F5`, secondary text `#A5B0BD`, quiet text `#6F7E90`, the empty ring of the Circle `#22364B`. Cards and fields are frosted (white at 7%, with a white 12% border), a solid card `#15263A` is used where something must hide what is behind it (the new-word card, the Map's info card), and small pills are white at 10%.
- **One accent: orange `#F75831`** for the main button, the active tab, the counter and its ring, and selected options. Text on it is white.
- **No red-to-green anywhere:** the Circle's rating is shown by how much its stars shine (blue and dim for "don't know" up to a golden blaze for "know"), not by a colour scale.
- Type is Satoshi with letter-spacing -1%, lowercase for the interface text (German words keep their capitals).
- The colors live in `src/theme.ts`. A proper visual identity comes later, once the author has collected references (Mobbin) and the interactions are proven.

- Run the Circle prototype on the iOS Simulator and on the author's iPhone. Check that the continuous scale feels natural and that the top snap is noticeable but not annoying.

- **Pressing the tab you are on goes back to its first level:** in Study it closes an open card, the form or the session and shows the list; in the Map it closes the open topic (the page of words and the card with it) and shows the whole sky; in Profile it closes the credits. In Explore there is nothing deeper, so nothing happens.
- **Moving between the three tabs is animated:** the old screen slides a little to the side, shrinks slightly and fades while the new one comes in from the side of the tab you pressed (about 0.4 s, easing out), and the orange tile in the tab bar glides to the new tab with a soft spring.
- **The Map reacts to the phone's tilt (gyroscope).** Tilting nudges the layers of the sky by different amounts, so it has depth (the small far stars move least, the big stars and their names most), and in an opened theme the sphere turns a little further and leans with the tilt. The tilt is measured from where the phone was held a moment ago (the zero slowly follows it) and smoothed. The sensor runs only while the Map is on screen, and not at all when the phone's Reduce Motion is on. Not available on the web.
- **Pinch to pull the camera back (with a snap).** Two fingers pulling together zoom the Map out, smoothly, down to a view of the **whole sky at once**; spreading them brings it back. As the camera pulls back the theme names fade out, so the far view shows only the stars and the threads between themes (the shape of the whole sky, and where it shines). While pulled back, tapping a star does nothing; pinch back in to open a theme. Dragging still works at every distance (and the sky is centred when it fits the screen). **The map only rests at two distances, close up and the whole sky:** while the fingers are down the map follows them, and when they lift it snaps (about a third of a second, with a light haptic tick) to the nearer of the two; a pinch that covered about a fifth of the way flips it, a smaller one springs back.
- **Camera mode (Map, phone only).** A small viewfinder button next to "words ›" turns the phone into a camera pointed at the sky: where the back of the phone points decides which part of the map is on screen, so the user finds a star by turning round like looking for it in the real sky. **The sky is above you**, so when the mode is switched on the view glides to the **bottom** of the map, and the phone has to be **raised** to climb (120 degrees of raising from the starting angle goes from the bottom of the map to the top); looking lower than where it started changes nothing. Sideways, 120 degrees of turning crosses the whole width. The view glides after the phone, and pinch still works. While it is on, dragging and the tilt parallax are off; it pauses while a theme is open (and afterwards carries on from the same view) and is off by default. There is no re-centre gesture (switch it off and on to start again).
- **The sky extends well past the outermost theme stars** (about half a screen of empty starry sky on every side), so no theme star sits near the edge of the sky, and the camera always has sky to look at beyond them.
